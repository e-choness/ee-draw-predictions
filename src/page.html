How scoring changed on November 3

A labour market integration factor worth up to 150 points now counts either Canadian work or a job offer, whichever scores higher. High-wage means the occupation's national median wage is at least 1.3, 1.5 or 2 times the average; 186 occupations qualify. An LMIA is no longer needed for a job offer to count.

IRCC does not publish the pool's occupations or Canadian work experience directly. The defaults use only figures that describe the whole pool or every profile submitted, never just the people already invited, who are a selected group.

  • High-wage occupations, about 56% of the pool: of the 218,291 candidates in the pool on December 1, 2024, 41,880 qualified for STEM, 17,066 for healthcare, 7,742 for trades, 7,673 for transport and 3,124 for agriculture. Matching each category's occupations to the high-wage list gives 96% for STEM, 57% healthcare, 80% transport, 20% trades and 0% agriculture. The other 140,806 candidates are counted at 45%, the high-wage share of the remaining 294 occupations open to Express Entry. Occupations within a group are weighted equally.
  • Points tiers: across the pool, high-wage candidates fall 15% in the 2× tier (+70), 52% in the 1.5× tier (+40) and 33% in the 1.3× tier (+20). Job offers use the same mix (100, 70 or 50 points).
  • Canadian work, about 36%: no pool-wide count exists. The closest is the 36% of all 339,621 eligible profiles submitted in 2024 that claimed Canadian education, most of whom gain Canadian work through post-graduation work permits.
  • Work points, 20% by default: 56% high-wage times 36% with Canadian work.
  • Job offers, 7% by default: 12.3% of eligible profiles in 2024 claimed job-offer points; at the pool's 56% high-wage share that is 6.9%. Some of these people also earn work points, and the model counts the two groups separately, so this default leans high.

Gains are spread evenly across score bands, and profiles entering during the pause are not counted. Only people with Canadian work can earn the work points, so Canadian Experience Class cut-offs may rise more than shown and other types less.

Sources: IRCC Express Entry Year-End Report 2024 (tables 3 and 17a, and the category occupation annex), the high-wage occupations list, and Statistics Canada's NOC 2021.

Method and sources

How every number on this dashboard is worked out.

The projection for a draw type adds three parts.

  1. Recent pattern. The average of two estimates on the old scores: a weighted average of the last six cut-offs (the newest counts most), and the score in IRCC's latest pool snapshot where a usual-size draw would run out. For the second, we measure what share of profiles at or above each recent cut-off were eligible and invited, and use the median share and median draw size of the last four draws. Profiles are assumed to be spread evenly within each IRCC score band.
  2. New points. Past cut-offs were set under the old rules. This step is the same pool estimate run on the pool with the November 3 points added under your scenario, minus the estimate on IRCC's last old-rules count. Once IRCC publishes a count dated November 3 or later, that count already uses the new rules and this step adds nothing.
  3. Draw size. The pool estimate at the draw size you set, minus the estimate at the usual size, both under the new rules.

The likely range comes from replaying the recent-pattern step on each type's last six draws and measuring how far off it was, widened by a quarter of the new-points and draw-size effects. The next date is the last draw plus the typical gap between draws of that type over the past year.

Provincial Nominee cut-offs sit above 600 and Physicians cut-offs below 301. IRCC reports those scores in one wide band each, so for those types only the recent-pattern step is used.

Data comes from IRCC's rounds of invitations feed and is refreshed daily. Rule details come from IRCC's 2026 changes page. These are statistical estimates, not IRCC guidance: IRCC picks each draw's size and type itself.
